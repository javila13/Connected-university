Testing drinking water for trace contaminants usually means expensive lab equipment that small towns and rural water systems can't afford. A team in Dr. Olga Martinez's chemistry lab is working to change that.

The team, which includes Environmental Science and Chemistry undergraduates, has developed a low-cost test that changes color in the presence of several common contaminants. Early results suggest it can flag problems that would otherwise go unnoticed between official lab tests.

"Our students did the hard work here," said Dr. Martinez, Associate Professor of Chemistry. "They designed the experiments, ran hundreds of trials and presented the results at our spring showcase."

The lab is now working with a regional water district to test the method in the field. Undergraduates interested in joining the lab can contact [Dr. Martinez](/academics/faculty/olga-martinez/), who also advises [Environmental Science](/academics/programs/environmental-science/) majors.
